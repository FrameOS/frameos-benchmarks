# main 9573236d4

A full bench of main `9573236d4` on every frame on the dev box rig, on 2026-11-26: every ESP32, the Pico, and both Raspberry Pis. It is compared with the full run of the 2026.11.15 release the night before (`20261125-232212`, [2026.11.15.md](2026.11.15.md)). Main is the release plus the fixes for that run's findings: frameos#1062, #1063, #1064 and #1065, plus flasher and cloud changes no frame runs.

- **Run:** `20261126-051523`, `bench.py run --full --rediscover --baseline 20261125-232212`, 2 h 07 min, every suite on every frame, nothing sampled.
- **Boards:** the six ESP32s run local builds of `9573236d4` (`firmware/build.sh`, ELF `f8b61cb0` generic and `bd334e2c` 32 MB), flashed one at a time with `flash_checked.sh`. Both images are 3,161,088 bytes, the same as the release's.
  - Since the release, the only firmware changes on the ESP32 side are `fos_console.c` and `cloud/hub_client.nim`. No render path changed.
- **Pico:** stays on `2026.11.12-main.e0a754bd0`. `embedded/pico` has not changed since the release.
- **Pis:** cross-built from `9573236d4` (`cross release`, arm64 for Cloud-5 and armv6 for Cloud-W, stamped `2026.11.15-main.9573236d4`).
  - Each is installed as a release directory of its own, `release_main_9573236d4`: a copy of the live release with the binary swapped. The drivers are the release's, since `driver_abi.nim` is unchanged.
  - Cloud-5's layout choice (macOS) was taken off for the run and put back after.
- **Backend:** the bench backend at `9573236d4` (no new migrations), with a wasm runtime built from it.
- **Verdict as printed:** 0 crash, 1 fail, 24 slow, 4 note. The fail is not a leak (see "Not regressions"), and none of the slow lines is a regression of main.

## Fixed, and confirmed on the boards

- **The Pi 5 no longer dies after a backend push** (frameos#1063). Its desktop suite (every layout pushed, four windows each) and its settings suite (a rotation and back, four layouts and none pushed in turn) ran with no SIGSEGV. On the release they had two crashes, and three in a settings suite with the macOS layout on. Stability 6.2 → 90.0, and the remaining 10 points are the false leak.
- **Sudoku draws on the Sticky** (frameos#1065): switch 1.8 s, cold 1.1 s, warm 103 ms, a tap in 504 ms. No stack overflow in its scenes run or its soak. On the release it errored on every render. Stability 0 → 100.
- **A push that restarts a Pi keeps its reply** (frameos#1062): every deploy of both Pis' settings suites succeeded, each rotation a single reload of under 0.9 s with a picture 1.0–1.6 s after the ask.
- **The bench's own fixes** (frameos#1064): no `switcher_drag` fail on the AMOLED (its switcher pages are drawn in bursts and counted now). No `not_in_place` on Cloud-5's scenes and desktops. No Kitchen sink "1.0 fps against 20" on the Pis.

Everything else is unchanged from the release. There was no crash, no fail and no issue on any ESP32 in the desktops, the code editor, the storm and the offline backend. The exception is the E1002's three `interpreter_discard` notes in the Tiling and Commander layouts, as on the release. On Cloud-W, video played all four clips on the hardware decoder onto the plane, with nothing late or dropped.

## Not regressions

- **Cloud-5 `leak` ("processRss rose every round, at the same pace: 65.6 → 68.3 MB"): the runtime settling.** The release's soak ended at the same place (63.5 → 68.8 MB, flagged only as settling because its first rise was bigger). A 14-round soak of Cloud-5 on the same build (`20261126-072426`, not recorded) climbed to 78.9 MB by round 10 and then dropped back to 68–70 MB: 61.1, 72.1, 72.3, 73.1, 74.2, 74.6, 88.2, 76.5, 76.8, 77.6, 78.9, 69.2, 68.4, 69.0, 69.6. It reported no issue. Five rounds are too few for this rule on a 4 GB Pi, whose allocator returns memory in large steps.
- **E1003 DVD logo bounce "fastest warm render 115 → 217 ms"** (the compare): the release's 115 ms was one render that redrew little (`scene` 236/216/115 ms). Now they are 231/222/217 ms. The median switch-to-done is 399 → 364 ms, and a quiet frame is 109.5 → 108 ms.
- **E1004 Flights above me "fastest warm render 3.7 → 8.4 s":** live flight data, run at 07:00 instead of 01:00. The JSX walk took 20 → 71 ms and the raster 2.8 → 6.7 s, which is more flights drawn, not a slower renderer. No render code changed.
- **Sticky Responsiveness 1283 → 1141 (−11 %), E1002 1176 → 1364 (+16 %):** both boards run the same render code as the release.
  - The Sticky's drop is two inputs: the scene switcher's open, a single sample (render 1384 → 2200 ms from the same scene), and the benchmark's median switch, 3957 → 4508 ms. Its cold switch waited 686 ms instead of 187 ms on the panel's previous refresh.
  - The E1002's gain is its boot to the first picture (12.6 → 8.3 s).
  - Both are e-paper refresh timing. Compare them again on the next run before calling either a change.
- **The AMOLED's `animation_fps`** (Color cycling 15.1 of 20 fps, Pixel toys 13.1 of 50 fps; a quiet frame of Pixel toys: scene 46 ms, of it app 42 and get 20.4): measured for the first time, since frameos#1064 made the AMOLED a fast panel. They are not a slowdown, but they are a lead: the SenseCAP, on the same S3, runs Pixel toys at 28.4 fps.
- **SenseCAP Slideshow with motion 11.7 fps and Pixel toys 28.4 fps:** 12.1 and 31.0 fps on the release.
- **E1002, E1004 and Sticky `restart_slow` (27–76 s) and the E1004's `render_slow` (11–37 s) and `code_render_slow` (26–35 s):** their e-paper refreshes, as on the release. Bird field journal joined the E1004's list at 11.6 s, against an 11 s line.
- **Notes:** the E1003's restart notice `no-memory` at rotate 90, Cloud-W's Video player switch not timed (the render log pauses after an animation), and the E1002's three interpreter discards. All three were in the release run too.

## Follow-ups (the same day)

- **The AMOLED's slow animations** were two costs on the render task, on top of the scene's own render:
  - **frameos#1070:** each present compared the frame buffers and pushed the changed box over 40 MHz QSPI, 31 ms for a whole frame, before the next frame could start. The push now runs on a task of its own.
  - **frameos#1069:** once a second, the housekeeping read the battery gauge with a 60 ms gap between two readings. An animation now reuses a reading up to 30 s old.
  - **Pixel toys 12.2 → 19.3 fps, Color cycling 18.5 → 19.9 fps** of its 20, measured on the board one build at a time. The benchmark suite's animation went 5.5 → 6.2 fps, with the renders themselves unchanged.
- **The Sticky's Responsiveness drop: not a regression.**
  - Release and main, flashed in turn on the board, overlap: median switch 3,957 / 4,709 ms against 4,508 / 4,660 / 3,957 ms; switcher open 1,681 / 2,104 ms against 2,494 / 3,005 / 1,804 ms.
  - `resp.switcher.open_ms` is one open, right after the benchmark suite replaced scenes. That makes the switcher read its layout off the 24 MB `/state` again: 1.6–2.5 s from the same build.
  - Underneath was a real cost, fixed in **frameos#1071**: the switcher opened over a scene started a second instance of that scene's layout. It now borrows the scene's own, and the Sticky keeps 150–170 KB more PSRAM free.
- **Bench: frameos#1072.** A booking of a board this checkout's `boards.json` does not know (a session bringing up a new board) crashed `bench.py run` for everyone.
- **Leads, not fixed:**
  - The first switcher open after a boot is 3.4 s of render on the Sticky. 1.8 s of it rasterizes 1,139 glyphs into an empty glyph cache.
  - After the desktop suite the Sticky holds about 1.8 MB more PSRAM than at the start. Main's soak shows it too: 3.77 MB free at round 0 against 5.6 MB idle.
- **Records:** the three Sticky A/B runs (`20261126-081832` the release, `-082559` and `-083452` main) are partial runs in `data/`.
