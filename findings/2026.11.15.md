# 2026.11.15

A full bench of the 2026.11.15 release on every frame on the dev box rig, on 2026-11-25. It ran on every ESP32, the Pico, and for the first time both Raspberry Pis: Cloud-W (a Pi Zero W) and Cloud-5 (a Pi 5). It is compared with the full run of main `ad692482e` the same morning (`20261125-104606`, [2026.11.14-main-ad692482e.md](2026.11.14-main-ad692482e.md)) and, for scores, with the 2026.11.14 release.

- **Run:** `20261125-232212`, `bench.py run --rediscover --full --baseline 20261125-104606`, 2 h 06 min, every suite on every frame, nothing sampled.
- **Boards:**
  - SenseCAP Indicator D1, reTerminal E1002, E1003 and E1004, and the AMOLED 1.75: flashed with the signed release images (`2026.11.15`, ELF `4a150fa8` generic / `fe5db032` 32 MB, signatures checked first).
  - The **reTerminal Sticky**, back on the rig: it came back standalone from the browser flasher, already on the release image, with 52 scenes. They were exported over USB into its backend row (frame 3) before it was attached.
  - The **Pico** stays on `2026.11.12-main.e0a754bd0`. A UF2 needs its BOOTSEL drive, and restarting it loses its console in this VM. Its firmware changed since (the security and triple-press commits), so it is not benched as 2026.11.15.
- **Pis:** both upgraded themselves with `frameos upgrade` (signature checked).
  - **Cloud-5** was adopted into the bench backend (`POST /frames/adopt`, frame 8). Its 27 cloud-pushed scenes were copied into it and its own layout choice (macOS) taken off.
  - **Cloud-W**'s first upgrade attempt refused hard-linked files (`refusing a file with 2 links`). Those came from test releases an earlier session made with `cp -al`, not from the product. Its live release got its own copies of those files, and the upgrade then went through.
- **Backend:** the bench backend at `v2026.11.15` (no new migrations since `ad692482e`), and the release's wasm tarball.
- **Verdict as printed:** 8 crash, 1 fail, 57 slow, 4 note.
  - **Real:** the Pi 5 died with SIGSEGV after a backend push (twice), and the Sticky's Sudoku never drew (a JS stack overflow). One more, found while setting up: a push that restarts a Pi lost its reply, and the backend failed a deploy the frame had applied.
  - **Bench bugs:** the fail and 31 of the slow lines. All are fixed (see "Fixed").

## Found

1. **A Pi died with SIGSEGV a quarter second after a backend push** (Cloud-5: twice in its desktop suite, at the Tiling and BeOS layouts). Replaying the bench's pushes on x86 with Cloud-5's own config and scenes reproduced it at the second push. ASan reported a heap-buffer-overflow in `orc.nim` `unregisterCycle`, with the runner freeing a Future callback while its own list of cycle candidates was empty.

   Underneath it is the same mechanism as frameos#1022, one level down. At boot the main thread reads the first generation of scenes and files their `DiagramNode`s and `DiagramEdge`s as cycle candidates in its own list: 155 of them for Cloud-5's set. They are inheritable refs, so ORC assumes they could be part of a cycle. At the second reload the runner frees that generation, and took each node out of *its own* list at the main thread's index. That overwrote the runner's own candidates, and the next callback it freed read past the end of its list.

   Any reload does it: a backend push, a cloud push or a save on the frame. **Fixed** in frameos#1063: both types are `{.acyclic.}`. With the fix, 18 replayed pushes leave nothing out of place and ASan reports nothing. On Cloud-5 itself, with its layout choice back on, the bench's settings suite crashed the release three times in one run and the fix build not at all in two. The desktop suite ran twice on the fix build without a crash. ESP32 image −288 bytes.
2. **The Sudoku sample never drew on an ESP32** (the Sticky, the only board with it): `JS app get failed: stack overflow` on every render. Making a puzzle counts solutions by recursion, one call per empty cell (50-odd for a medium puzzle). QuickJS takes a C stack frame per JS call, and an ESP32 gives it 20 KB (`FOS_JS_STACK_SIZE`). **Fixed** in frameos#1065: the same search with its levels kept in arrays. It gives the same counts on 16,000 grids and the same puzzle for every seed and difficulty, and the Sticky renders the scene in 1.0 s.
3. **A push that restarts a Pi cut its own reply off, and the backend failed a deploy the frame had applied** (found while setting up, at Cloud-5's adoption): `peer closed connection without sending complete message body (received 108600 bytes, expected 165193)`. The save queued the runner's `restart` (a `quit`) before it even built its reply. **Fixed** in frameos#1062: the save and `POST /api/frames/1/restart` reply first and restart 1.5 s later, as the ESP32 has always done (`restart_task`, 750 ms). On Cloud-5 the restart route answered in 7 ms and restarted 1.50 s later, and every deploy of a settings suite succeeded.

## Not regressions

The four bench bugs are fixed in frameos#1064, and the same suites on the AMOLED and Cloud-5 with it have no issue.

- **The AMOLED's `switcher_drag` fail and its 30 s refresh: a bench bug.** The bench treated it as e-paper (`lcd` was only a display id starting `LCD_`), so it waited 80 s for a drag and skipped the animation benchmark. Separately, the switcher pages waited for a `render #N done`, while an LCD sums quick renders in one `render:burst` line. Page 2 was drawn five times in a second and read as 59.8 s, and the switcher closed itself meanwhile. Now `AMOLED_` and boards.json's `kind: lcd` count as fast, and a burst counts as drawn. **The AMOLED's benchmark workload grows from its next run.**
- **`not_in_place` on all 22 of Cloud-5's scenes and 9 desktops: a bench bug.** On Linux, `in place` is the framebuffer's depth, not a path a render took. The Pi 5's HyperPixel is 32 bpp KMS, which has no RGB565 panel canvas.
- **Kitchen sink "1.0 fps against a 20 fps target" on both Pis: a bench bug.** Outside its Motion page the scene asks for one render a second (`frameos.setNextSleep`). The Linux target is now the scene's own `render:sleep` when it is longer than its interval.
- **SenseCAP Kitchen sink "fastest warm render 42 → 185 ms"** (the compare): the warm renders were 357/42/212 ms on main and 332/212/185 ms now. The 42 was one render that changed little.
- **E1004 Wikimedia Commons 8.4 → 10.8 s:** a photo downloaded live each render.
- **E1002/E1004 `restart_slow` (30–48 s) and the E1004's `render_slow` and `code_render_slow` (11–36 s):** their Spectra refreshes, as on main (E1002 51/30 s, E1004 75/34 s then).
- **SenseCAP Slideshow with motion 12.1 fps and Pixel toys 31 fps:** the same 12.1 fps, and 19.7 fps before (30.8 when measured again). Cloud-W's slideshow at 7.1 fps is its single core.
- **Notes:** the E1003's restart notice `no-memory` at rotate 90 (seen before), and Cloud-5's resident set settling over its soak (63.5 → 68.8 MB, slower each round).
